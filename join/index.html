PhD Students

The AltMem lab is hiring graduate students in the upcoming cycle, matriculating Fall 2027. The deadline for applications is November 20th, 2026, 10pm MT.

Detailed instructions are found on the department’s website.

Below are some ideal qualifications. Successful candidates will have significant (but not necessarily complete) overlap with these criteria:

  • Bachelor’s or Master’s degree in a relevant field (e.g., Psychology, Neuroscience, or Computational/Computer Science)
  • A strong interest in studying learning and memory in humans
  • Research experience with human subjects
  • Technical ability (e.g., data analysis with Python/R)

Regardless of past research experience, potential graduate students should be able to demonstrate critical scientific thinking. For example, it is one thing to be involved in data collection for a study. It is another to be able to articulate the scientific goal of the study (i.e., the hypothesis), and how the approach (i.e., methods) was appropriate to accomplish that goal. This applies to all levels of previous research experience. In our lab the quality of science is measured by the rigor of its execution.

The research interests of the lab are fairly broad. However, almost all of our work connects back to these central themes: How are emotional experiences represented in the brain? What are the basic cognitive and neurobiological mechanisms of memory (episodic or associative, neutral or valenced)? How do these processes contribute to psychiatric disorders? Can we develop novel interventions to improve mental-health related outcomes?

As a neuroscience lab, a central tenet of our research goals is to understand how the brain supports cognition and behavior. As such, trainees are expected to use neuroimaging to inform their research. The lab’s primary neuroimaging tools are fMRI and EEG, however this could also include different modalities (e.g., fNIRS, MEG) collected in partnership with collaborators, or re-analyses of previously collected datasets.

I strongly encourage you to not use AI to generate any part of your application, including emails. The lab has a strict policy against using AI in the generation phase of written content. Writing (and by extension thinking) is a core component of scientific training and practice, and should not be outsourced. This policy is designed to protect you as a trainee, giving you space to develop your own writing process and scientific voice.

This is not a blanket policy; LLMs are extremely useful tools for the process of science (e.g., coding), and their use is encouraged for non-writing tasks.
